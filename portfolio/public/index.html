Production · EventLinx · 2026

Seating-chart detection

A vision service that converts an image of a venue's seating chart into a structured, machine-readable seat map.

  • 5 / 5benchmark venues
  • $0.91per map
  • 250+real maps benchmarked

Charts with seat count in range

Single call
Cheaper tiles
Strong tiles
Model development
Trained and fine-tuned YOLO11 and a custom ResNet18 CNN on GPUs with real and synthetic seat maps, benchmarked against 250+ real venue maps.
Architecture change
Replaced the trained pipeline with a single Azure OpenAI vision service once it measured better: expected seat counts on all 5 benchmark venues.
Rejected designs
A single-call design put 1 of 6 charts in range, and a cheaper tile model 0 of 7 against 5 of 7. Both were dropped on the evidence.

Pipeline
Public immigration, housing and education data integrated into a 4,188 × 41 feature set of lags, rolling means and calendar terms, served through a Power BI model with what-if parameters.
Model selection
Prophet with exogenous regressors, RandomForest and XGBoost compared per province on a chronological 80/20 holdout and selected by RMSE. Prophet won 8 of 9 regions.
What went wrong
A pooled model made RandomForest look strong at R² 0.84. Fitted per province it lost in every region, so pooling was dropped.

Architecture
React Native on Expo with SQLite in WAL mode, on-device speech recognition in 6 locales, and a rule-based NLP engine for segmentation and entity, date and urgency extraction.
Classifier
A hybrid context classifier: weighted rules, prototype similarity and feedback from user corrections, with feedback capped per token and confidence clamped to a fixed band.
Verification
325 Jest tests and 16 Maestro device flows, including accessibility regression and seeded 5,000-item stress runs, validated on a physical device.
What went wrong
Three architectures were built and removed: a Gemini cloud parser, a Flutter rewrite and a self-hosted Phi-3 server. The shipped engine is deterministic and fully on-device.

BogdAI contract risk review

Tests I wrote

Mine
Architecture
An orchestrator runs six agents in sequence, from intake to reporting. The report is validated against 9 Pydantic models, frozen as the contract for a teammate's interface.
Design
A deterministic 7-rule engine decides which flags exist. The LLM only enriches each flag in JSON mode, and degrades per call to the rule output on failure.
Guardrails
A verifier agent checks every finding for a citation and evidence, forces human review on high-risk flags and scans for unsafe-advice phrasing. The full agent trace is returned.
Limits
Synthetic contracts only, no evaluation of LLM output, and recall bounded by the seven rules. I wrote the backend; a teammate built the API and interface.

    anonymized · draft for reviewPolicy constraints, escalation behavior, reproducible investigations and durable workflows.

    Owner review required. This case-study draft uses illustrative technical practices based on the requested themes. Each proposed personal contribution below needs confirmation. No private artifacts or identifying details are included.

    The problem

    A plausible answer can still cross a policy boundary, lose an escalation or repeat a defect. Reliable behavior needs explicit decisions, inspectable evidence and a workflow that survives handoffs.

    Policy constraints

    Define permitted actions, required evidence and approval conditions before execution. Separate actions that may proceed, prohibited actions that stay blocked, and ambiguous decisions that need human review. Escalation alone grants no permission.

    Review required — proposed personal contribution: I translated policy requirements into explicit constraints and boundary scenarios.

    Escalation behavior

    Pause the affected action when policy is ambiguous or evidence is insufficient. Give a reviewer the trigger, minimum necessary context and a clear decision request. Missing acknowledgement stays pending. Resume only after a recorded decision and a fresh policy check.

    Review required — proposed personal contribution: I investigated escalation behavior and clarified when a workflow must pause, hand off, and resume.

    Reproducible defect investigation

    Reduce a report to synthetic inputs, fixed preconditions, configuration and policy versions, exact steps, and expected versus observed behavior. Repeat runs to capture variability. Separate observations from hypotheses, isolate one variable at a time, and test both the failing case and permitted behavior after a fix.

    Review required — proposed personal contribution: I built reproducible defect reports using minimal examples, expected versus observed behavior, and regression checks.

    Durable workflow design

    Use a versioned playbook with entry criteria, decision rules, ownership, evidence requirements and closure criteria. Preserve pending decisions and completion state through interruptions. Make retries safe to repeat without duplicate effects, and retain each confirmed defect as a regression scenario.

    Review required — proposed personal contribution: I designed durable workflow guidance that preserves decisions, evidence, and ownership across handoffs.

    Illustrative scenario: a retry loses the review gate

    Synthetic example, not an incident record: an ambiguous request should pause for review, but a retry continues without a decision. Replay the same request and pending state, inspect the decision path, and isolate the transition that lost the gate.

    Acceptance check: pending and denied decisions stay stopped across retries and restarts. An approved action resumes only after rechecking policy and produces no duplicate effects.

    Intended value and limits

    A reviewer can understand the pause, another investigator can rerun the defect, and a maintainer can continue the workflow. These are intended benefits. No measured improvement, deployment status or business outcome is claimed. Confirm individual versus team scope and which practices reflect completed work.
